# Skill 03: Situational Judgment Test (SJT) Engineering & Psychometrics

## 1. Introduction to SJT Items
**Situational Judgment Tests (SJTs)** form the core of the SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP certification exams. Unlike knowledge-recall questions, SJTs measure **judgment, procedural decision-making, and competency application** in ambiguous workplace scenarios.

A well-crafted SJT item does **not** test memory; it evaluates how an HR professional prioritizes competing priorities, applies employment law, balances employee advocacy with business objectives, and navigates power dynamics.

---

## 2. Anatomy of an Authentic SHRM SJT Item

Every high-quality SJT question comprises 5 foundational elements:
1. **Organizational Context**: Industry, company size, business stage (e.g. high-growth tech startup, unionized manufacturer, acquired retail unit).
2. **Key Actors & Stakes**: Relevant stakeholders (e.g., tenured supervisor, complaining subordinate, C-suite executive, HR Director).
3. **The Core Dilemma**: Tension between policy, interpersonal dynamics, business deadlines, or legal compliance.
4. **The Strategic Prompt**:
   - `"What is the FIRST action HR should take?"` $\to$ Evaluates process sequencing and fact-finding.
   - `"What is the BEST strategic recommendation HR should make?"` $\to$ Evaluates root-cause resolution and executive partnership.
5. **Four High-Plausibility Options (A–D)**: All choices must appear professional and defensible to an untrained manager; only one strictly satisfies SHRM BASK principles.

---

## 3. The 4 Psychometric Distractor Traps

Standard multiple-choice tests often feature "cartoonishly bad" distractors (e.g., *"Immediately fire the employee"*, *"Ignore the issue"*). On official SHRM exams, **such obvious distractors do not exist**. 

All 4 options must be written as defensible, professional actions. Their incorrectness stems from subtle psychometric traps:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     THE 4 SHRM DISTRACTOR TRAPS                        │
├────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Sequence Trap (Wrong Timing)│ A valid action, but performed too     │
│                                │ early or out of sequential order.     │
├────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Common Sense Trap           │ Seems intuitive/practical, but breaks │
│                                │ an established SHRM compliance rule.  │
├────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Escalation Trap             │ Over-escalates (e.g., bypassing to    │
│                                │ Board) or under-escalates (isolated). │
├────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Symptomatic / Band-Aid Trap │ Treats the visible surface symptom,   │
│                                │ ignoring the structural root cause.   │
└────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Trap 1: The Sequence Trap ("Right Action, Wrong Timing")
*Scenario:* An employee alleges supervisor harassment with unnamed witnesses.
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Schedule a joint meeting with the supervisor to confront them with the allegations.* (Legitimate step, but premature; tips off the supervisor before witness statements are secured).
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Conduct a document audit of the supervisor's historical evaluations.* (Good context, but secondary to securing witness accounts).
- ✅ **Correct Answer**: *Confidential witness interviews individually before notifying the accused.*

### Trap 2: The Common Sense Trap ("Managerial Intuition vs. SHRM Principle")
*Scenario:* Sales manager asks to dock remote employees' pay if their webcams are turned off.
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Approve pay docking if documented in the employee handbook.* (Sounds like management rights, but violates wage and hour statutes; wage theft).
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Provide a technology stipend to buy better webcams.* (Generous, but ignores privacy and caregiver concerns).
- ✅ **Correct Answer**: *Advise against wage deductions and establish flexible virtual meeting norms.*

### Trap 3: The Escalation Trap ("Wrong Level of Intervention")
*Scenario:* Two Senior VPs are in an escalating turf war; the CEO refuses to intervene.
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Escalate directly to the Board of Directors with turnover metrics.* (Premature; bypasses the CEO before formal executive business cases are delivered).
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Monitor passively until the CEO changes their mind.* (Dereliction of duty; permits continued talent flight).
- ✅ **Correct Answer**: *Re-engage the CEO with quantified business impact data (turnover costs, project delays) framing conflict resolution as an operational necessity.*

### Trap 4: The Symptomatic / Band-Aid Trap
*Scenario:* Incumbent software engineers threaten to resign over pay compression.
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Offer incumbent engineers a one-time spot bonus.* (Solves immediate anger, but base pay structure remains compressed).
- ❌ **Distractor**: *Freeze new-hire starting salaries until the market stabilizes.* (Damages external recruiting competitiveness).
- ✅ **Correct Answer**: *Conduct a comprehensive salary equity audit and adjust base salary bands.*

## 4. Quality Control & Anti-Guessing Architecture

### 4.1 Option Length Parity
A common flaw in AI-generated quizzes is that the correct answer is significantly longer and more detailed than the distractors. 

**Rule of Parity:**
$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{\min(\text{length})}{\max(\text{length})} \ge 0.65$$
All 4 options must maintain comparable character lengths and syntactical complexity.

### 4.2 Runtime Option Shuffling
In `index.html`, options A, B, C, D are dynamically mapped at runtime through Fisher-Yates shuffle:
```javascript
function prepareQuestionChoices(q) {
  const indices = [0, 1, 2, 3];
  shuffle(indices);
  quiz.currentChoices = indices;
}
```
This guarantees that learners evaluate the **substance of each choice**, preventing memorization of option letters.
